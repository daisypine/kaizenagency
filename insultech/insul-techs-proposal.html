Site review, September 30, 2026

What's on the site now, and what I'd do about it

I went back through insultechs.com today. The site runs on WordPress with a purchased theme called Industrie, plus Slider Revolution, a translation plugin for the Spanish version, Smartsupp for chat, and Google Site Kit. That's all workable. None of this needs a teardown.

This list is the cleanup work. It's what months one through three go toward.

  • Open since July

    Homepage title tag just says "Insul Techs inc"

    Why it matters: this is the line Google shows in search results. It's the highest-leverage field on the whole site and right now it says nothing about what you do or where.

    What I'd do: rewrite it to lead with the service and the area, the way your competitor's does. Then do the same across every page.

  • Open since July

    Meta description runs about 600 characters

    Why it matters: Google cuts off around 160. Everything past that is invisible, and stuffing it reads as spam.

    What I'd do: rewrite every meta description to fit, written for a person deciding whether to click rather than for a search engine.

  • New find

    Theme demo copy is still live on the homepage

    Why it matters: the About block renders twice, and one copy still reads "Welcome to Industrie, a leading industry innovator... empowering industries and driving progress for over 30 years." That's placeholder text that shipped with the theme.

    What I'd do: remove it, resolve the duplicate block, and write the About section properly using your actual history.

  • New find

    Two different business addresses on the same page

    Why it matters: the header and one footer say Martinez. The FAQ and the other footer say Concord. Google reads the contradiction and trusts neither, and this is a direct local ranking factor.

    What I'd do: pick one official address with you, then make it match across the site, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Diamond Certified and BBB.

  • Open since July

    Headings don't follow any structure

    Why it matters: "URGENT MESSAGE" is carrying heading weight on the homepage, and the services page H1 just says "Services." Blog posts are mostly tagged H3 with no H1 above them. Google can't tell what any page is about.

    What I'd do: give every page a real H1 that names the service and the area, then rebuild the H2s and H3s underneath it so there's a clear hierarchy.

  • New find

    Fireplaces and Demolition sit on separate subdomains

    Why it matters: Google treats them as different websites, so anything they earn doesn't help insultechs.com. They're also each built as one long scrolling page, which makes it near impossible to rank for something specific like commercial demolition.

    What I'd do: move them onto the main domain as folders, then break each one into individual pages. Residential, commercial and industrial demolition can each rank on their own.

  • New find

    Internal training documents are publicly indexed

    Why it matters: your capacitacion and induction document pages are sitting in the public sitemap, which means Google can crawl and surface internal onboarding material.

    What I'd do: pull them out of the sitemap and set them to noindex.

  • New find

    456 backlinks from 242 domains, authority score of 7

    Why it matters: that's a lot of links producing very little authority, which usually means they're low quality or unrelated.

    What I'd do: audit the link profile before anyone spends effort building more.

  • New find

    Most service pages untouched since 2024

    Why it matters: the sitemap shows the majority last modified in late 2024 or early 2025. These are the pages that should be earning the commercial and transactional rankings, and they're the ones getting no attention.

    What I'd do: go through them one by one. Keep, rewrite, merge or remove, then rebuild the ones worth building.

  • New find

    Smaller cleanups

    Why it matters: two Instagram handles across the site, a contact@ address that actually links to info@, a footer copyright that still says 2024, and an English FAQ linking to the Spanish privacy policy.

    What I'd do: tidy all of it. None of it is hard, it just needs someone going through it.

The Spanish version runs on a translation plugin, so changes made to the English pages carry across automatically. Worth knowing so nothing gets done twice.

Two things. Domain access, because that's where the DNS lives and it's what connects Search Console. And WordPress admin, because that's where the build, the copy and the photos are. They're separate logins and I'd need both.

I'll handle it. It runs on a translation plugin, so most of what gets updated in English carries across automatically. Where it needs a human, I speak Spanish, so it doesn't need routing to anyone else.

No. The theme works and everything on the list is a fix, not a teardown. Once the cleanup is done there's room in the back half to modernize the design, and that's included rather than a separate project.

When do we actually see something?

Technical fixes show up in Search Console within a few weeks. The keywords sitting in positions 51 to 100 have the most room to move and that usually starts landing around months two to three. New pages take four to six months to mature. Anyone promising faster is either buying ads or guessing.
