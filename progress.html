The Ranking Question — An Honest Answer

The site has been live for four weeks. Four weeks is not a result yet — and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

Here's what's actually happening, in plain terms:

1. Google is still re-reading the site

When a site is rebuilt this substantially, Google has to re-crawl it, re-evaluate every page, and rebuild its understanding of what the business does. For a rebuild of this size the normal re-evaluation window is three to six months. We are at week four.

2. You rank because of pages — and most of the pages aren't published yet

This is the most important point. Nobody ranks for "commercial painting San Jose" without a page about commercial painting in San Jose. Right now six pages are live. The roughly ninety pages that target the searches your customers actually type — each city, each industry, each service — are researched, mapped, and largely written, but they aren't on the site yet.

That gap is the single biggest lever we have, and closing it is the entire focus of the next sixty days.

3. What we removed was suppressing you

The cleanup matters even though it isn't visible. Demo template pages competing with your real pages, empty portfolio URLs, duplicate project layouts, and tag pages pointing at the wrong content all told Google the site was low-quality and confusing to crawl. Removing that doesn't produce an overnight jump — it removes the ceiling.

What to measure right now

At week four, rankings are the wrong scoreboard. The right ones are indexation (how many pages Google has accepted), impressions (how often you're being shown at all), and crawl coverage. Those move first; rankings follow, then calls. Search Console and Analytics are being set up now so we have a documented baseline from the launch date forward — which also means every future report shows real numbers, not opinions.

Active Right Now — This Week

  • Google Search Console & Analytics 4 setup. Verify the domain, submit the sitemap, and establish the baseline so every future report is measurable.
  • Sitemap corrections. Two issues found in the current setup: your blog and project posts aren't included in the sitemap being sent to Google, and a leftover WordPress "sample page" is. Both get fixed this week so Google sees the real inventory.
  • Old-URL redirect map. Making sure every legacy URL points somewhere useful, so nothing that already had value on Google gets dropped.
  • Internal linking on the Industries and Locations pages. Both currently list your verticals and cities as plain text. As each child page publishes, it gets linked — which is how authority flows through the site.
  • Google Business Profile optimization. Categories, services, service areas, and photos aligned to the new site. For local painting searches this is frequently the fastest-moving lever available.
