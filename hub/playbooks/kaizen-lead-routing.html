Kaizen Lead Routing + Form Protection

Goal: every real lead lands in the client's inbox within a minute, never in spam, and bots/scam submissions never reach them. Works for Elementor Pro Forms and Fluent Forms on SiteGround.

Ask first

  1. Which inbox should get leads? (client's main email; who else?)

  2. Does the client have Google Workspace / Microsoft 365 for their domain? Can we get a website@<domain> sender or admin access?

  3. Do they use a CRM / booking tool (Housecall Pro, Jobber, Jane, Calendly) that leads should also reach?

  4. Is Cloudflare in front of the site, or only SiteGround DNS? Any change to a live form needs Daisy's OK in words that match the change.

1. Where leads go

  • To: the client's inbox. BCC Kaizen ([Kaizen ops Google account - ask Daisy]) for the first 30 days to catch misses, then remove the BCC (calendar a reminder).

  • Reply-To: the visitor's email field, so the client can hit Reply.

  • Subject: real and specific, e.g. New quote request - <Client> website / New commercial bid request - <Client> website. Never "New message from WordPress".

  • Body: every field labelled, plus page URL the form was sent from.

  • Separate forms for homeowners (Get a Quote) and commercial (Request a Commercial Bid) so the client can filter.

2. Deliverability (so leads don't land in spam)

  • Send through SMTP (FluentSMTP) from an address on the client's domain, ideally website@<domain>. Never send "from" the visitor's address.

  • DNS on the client's domain: one SPF record including the sender (merge includes, never two SPF records), DKIM enabled for that sender, DMARC at least p=none with a report address.

  • Send one real test per form from a phone, outside the office network; confirm it lands in the client's inbox (not spam/promotions) and the Reply-To works.

3. Spam and bot protection

  • Honeypot field on every form (Elementor: add the Honeypot field; Fluent Forms: enable honeypot in settings).

  • Cloudflare Turnstile on every form (free, invisible to most people). Prefer it over reCAPTCHA.

  • Only require the fields the client needs (name, phone or email, message/scope). Fewer required fields = fewer abandoned real leads.

  • Block obvious junk: links in the message field, non-local phone formats if the client only serves locally (use with care), and known spam domains.

  • Keep Wordfence on; enable rate limiting.

  • Don't put the client's email address in plain text on the page; use the form or a click-to-email that's obfuscated.

4. After submit

  • Thank-you page (/thank-you/) with the booking link (e.g. Calendly) and what happens next. Fire the GA4 conversion on that page (after consent).

  • Optional auto-reply to the visitor from the client's domain, short and personal.

  • Log submissions (Elementor Submissions / Fluent entries) and, if no CRM, a Google Sheet log.

5. Test + record

  • Submit both forms, check: inbox, subject, reply-to, thank-you page, GA4 event, no spam folder.

  • Record what changed in the client's Cleanup Log (sitemap hub) and move the board card to Done.

  • 30 days later: remove the Kaizen BCC, check spam volume, tighten if needed.

Don't

  • Don't change where leads go without Daisy's OK.

  • Don't enter or store passwords; the client or Daisy signs in.

  • Don't route leads only to an agency inbox long-term.
