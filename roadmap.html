The single biggest opportunity in this document

Twenty-five years of customers, never marketed to

You've been in business since 2001. Interior repaints cycle every 5–7 years; exteriors every 7–10. Every customer you painted between roughly 2016 and 2020 is due right now — and none of them have ever been contacted. That list is sitting in QuickBooks. It costs almost nothing to activate, and it converts better than anything we can buy.

Why the timing matters

Q1 — January through March — is your slow season, and this spring was already unusually slow with competitors undercutting by $3,000–$5,000. Direct mail, email, and reactivation campaigns take 60–90 days to build and warm up. Build this September through December and Q1 2027 has something to sell into instead of waiting for the phone. Building it in January is too late.

The Foundation Underneath All of It — Brand Consistency

Before any of the channels below are worth spending on, everything has to look like it came from the same company. A postcard that doesn't match the website doesn't build recognition — it splits it.

That's now handled. A complete Sunwest brand system has been documented — the signature yellow-to-orange gradient, the full color palette, the Outfit and Poppins type system, button and card specifications, photography direction, and voice guidelines. Every value was pulled from the approved website design, so print, mail, signage, and digital now all reference one source.

What this unlocks

Any vendor can produce on-brand work without a design round

Sign shops, print vendors, and mail houses can be handed the guide and produce work that matches — no back-and-forth, no approximations, no "close enough" orange. Both typefaces are free through Google Fonts, so there's no license to buy and no vendor excuse for substituting something else.

One fix included

The audit turned up a real accessibility issue: white text on the brand orange measures 2.97:1 contrast, well below the 4.5:1 standard. That's the primary "Request a Quote" button — the hardest-working element on the site, and the least readable, especially on a phone outdoors. Switching to near-black on the gradient measures 7.06:1 and looks more premium. It's a one-line change that applies everywhere at once.

Print & Physical Presence

Door Hangers

Designed & readyFastest to launch

The highest-return piece of paper in the painting business, and yours are already designed and sitting in Drive. Your crews are already on the street — they just need something in their hands.

  • Two versions, not one. A "we're working on your street this week" drop before the job starts, and a "we just finished your neighbor's home" drop with a before-and-after photo when it wraps.
  • 50–100 doors per job site. Crews drop them at start and finish. No media spend, no list, no lead time.
  • Unique QR code per neighborhood so we can measure which routes produce calls instead of guessing.

Direct Mail

Build in 30 days

Four distinct campaigns, ranked by expected return. USPS Every Door Direct Mail runs $0.26 per piece in postage with no permit or annual fee; all-in with printing, budget roughly $0.32–$0.41 per piece depending on size and quantity.

  • Job-site radius mailer. 150–250 homes around every active job: "We're painting on your street." Neighbors have watched your crew work for two weeks — the highest-converting mail piece available to you.
  • Past-customer repaint reminder. "It's been seven years since we painted your home." Targeted at the 2016–2020 cohort. The cheapest revenue you will ever generate.
  • EDDM to high-ticket carrier routes. Los Gatos, Saratoga, Almaden Valley, Willow Glen, Palo Alto — the areas carrying your higher residential ticket. No list purchase required; USPS targets by carrier route.
  • Property manager & HOA postcard. A targeted list, quarterly touch, driving to the commercial bid page. Commercial pricing is consistent across cities, so geography matters less and repetition matters more.

Flyers, Folders & One-Pagers

Designed & ready

Your flyer, trifold, and presentation folder are done and waiting on sign-off. They're built for in-person prospecting — the channel where you're already strong through BNI.

  • Property manager drop-off packet. Folder + trifold + one-pager, hand-delivered to management companies. This is how commercial accounts actually get won in this trade.
  • Realtor pre-sale flyer. Realtors need fast, reliable pre-listing paint jobs and refer the same painter repeatedly. A referral channel you aren't tapping.
  • BNI and networking leave-behind — one-pager with a QR to a dedicated landing page, so referrals are trackable.
  • Every piece gets its own QR and landing page. Print without tracking is a guess; print with tracking is a channel.

Posters, Signage & Vehicles

Cheapest recurring ad

You already occupy physical space across the Bay Area every single day. Right now it isn't branded, and it isn't working for you.

  • Yard signs at every active residential job. Ask permission at contract signing so it's automatic. A sign sitting out for two weeks in Los Gatos pays for itself many times over. Black on the brand yellow is the most visible combination in the palette.
  • Job-site banners on commercial projects. Weeks of exposure on a high-traffic building, seen by exactly the facilities and property managers you want.
  • Vehicle wraps or magnetic panels. Your crews drive the peninsula daily. Free impressions you're currently giving away.
  • Community and trade posters — hardware stores, community boards, and HOA clubhouses where posting is permitted.

Email & Text — Reaching People You Already Know

Email Marketing

Build in 30–60 days

Mailchimp was used previously and nothing is active now. Five sequences, built once, then running on their own.

  • Past-customer reactivation. The flagship. Segment your QuickBooks history by job year and service type, then run repaint-cycle campaigns against it.
  • Estimate follow-up sequence. You lose bids on price. A structured three-touch follow-up — value, not discount — recovers a real percentage of "we went with someone cheaper," because the cheap bid often falls apart.
  • Property manager nurture. A monthly email that's genuinely useful: maintenance calendars, budget planning for the next fiscal year, compliance notes. Stay in the inbox until the repaint cycle comes around.
  • Post-project sequence. Thank you → review request → referral ask → repaint reminder scheduled years out. Fully automated.
  • Seasonal campaigns. Exterior season in spring, interior season in fall and winter, and a Q1 program to fill your slow months.

SMS / Text

Pairs with CRM

Text response rates run dramatically higher than email for short, transactional messages. Used sparingly it's the highest-performing channel you have. Used carelessly it's a compliance problem — so we build it with written opt-in from the start.

  • Estimate confirmations and reminders — cuts no-shows on site visits, which are pure lost time for your estimator.
  • Review requests after final walkthrough. Texted review requests substantially outperform emailed ones, and this directly feeds your local search rankings.
  • Project update texts. Communication and updates are your stated differentiator versus owner-operators. Right now that's manual. Automating it means it happens on every job, without anyone remembering.
  • Consent handled properly — opt-in captured at contract, documented, with easy opt-out.

The CRM — The Backbone Everything Plugs Into

This is the piece that makes all of the above possible instead of theoretical.

Today the business runs on QuickBooks and memory. There's no pipeline, no automated follow-up, no way to segment past customers, and no reporting. With your parents moving toward retirement in two to three years and the goal being a business that runs like a machine rather than relying on family, this is the specific thing that gets you there.

Sequencing note

The CRM should come before the email and text programs, not after. Email and SMS run off the customer database — building them first means building them twice.

Reviews & Google Business Profile

Worth calling out separately because it's the fastest-moving lever in this entire document. Local painting searches are won largely on the map pack, and the map pack runs on review volume, review recency, and profile completeness — all of which move in weeks, not months.

  • Restart the review engine. Automated request after every final walkthrough, to Google and Yelp, by text and email.
  • We respond to reviews on your behalf. Response rate is both a ranking signal and a trust signal to anyone reading.
  • Full profile optimization — categories, services, service areas, and a steady stream of project photos.
  • Reactivate the backlog. Every satisfied customer from the last two years is a review you never asked for.
