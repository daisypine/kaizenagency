Questions you probably have

What happens to my current site while you build?

It stays live and working the whole time. Nothing changes for your customers until we switch the new one on, and that happens with your say-so.

How many pages do I actually get?

Seven to start, and up to ten at the same price. Seven covers every service you offer with a real page behind each one, which is the jump that matters most. The other three get added once we can see what people are actually searching for, usually as city pages. Building all ten on day one is how people end up with pages nobody reads.

What do I actually get at launch?

Every login: domain, hosting, WordPress, Search Console and Analytics. Plus a walkthrough so you can change text and photos yourself. It's built on WordPress, which any web developer can pick up, so you are never stuck waiting on one person.

What does the $500 a year actually cover?

The server the site runs on, WordPress and plugin updates, security monitoring, off-site backups and uptime checks. It's free for your first full year. After that it's $500 annually or $50 monthly, cancel whenever. If you ever want to move it elsewhere I'll export everything and help you do it.

Will this put me at the top of Google?

Nobody can promise that. What I can tell you is the specific gap: you have 480+ reviews and sit at #18 for the main search in your own city, because Google has one page from you covering eight services. Giving it seven real pages to read is the part that's in our control.

How long before I see anything change?

New pages usually take a few months to settle into position, so this isn't a next-week thing. What changes at launch is what you can see: how the site looks, what it says about your work, and your reviews finally being visible on it.

How much work is this for me?

About an hour and a half total. One recorded call, one read-through of the copy, one look at the design, one final walkthrough. Everything else is on me.

Do I need to worry about California privacy rules?

A little, yes. California's privacy laws apply to business websites that use analytics or tracking, and the demand letters that go out tend to target small businesses with no cookie consent and no privacy policy. Your current site has neither. Getting that set up correctly is included here, so it's handled at launch rather than something you deal with later.

What's not included?

Google or Facebook ads, pages beyond the tenth, ongoing monthly SEO and blog content, the CRM subscription itself if you decide to buy one, and printing costs for your marketing pieces. All available later, none needed to launch.
